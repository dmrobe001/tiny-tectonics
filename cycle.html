An assembly is counted when the largest cluster holds at least 90% of the continents after having dropped below 70%. The angle is between the mean continental positions of successive assemblies.

A deliberately minimal model, not the physics model. Continents are rigid disks doing overdamped Brownian motion down the gradient of a low-degree mantle temperature field. Each continent quickly cools the mantle beneath it (slabs sinking at its margins, short memory τc), which draws other continents in, and slowly heats it (insulation, long memory τ), which eventually drives them apart. Insulation scales with the width of the blanket, so only a large cluster heats the mantle strongly. Fast attraction, slow repulsion.
